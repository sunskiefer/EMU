303MPC 0.2.1 (pre-release, offline build only). Roland TB-303 emulation for Akai Force / MPC OS. **Not confirmed working on a Force or any MPC yet.**

New in 0.2.1: the panel is now the author's own art (the earlier panel is gone). Every control sits on the art and is touchable: 10 knobs that turn in place, the WAVEFORM switch (its tab slides between Saw and Square), the 13 note keys, DOWN, UP, ACCENT, SLIDE, FUNCTION, PITCH MODE, TIME MODE, PATTERN CLEAR, BACK, RUN/STOP and TAP. Pressing a button draws an orange highlight on its cap; the panel's LEDs stay as drawn. The socket labels were removed from the panel. The sound is unchanged from 0.2.0.

Three zips: `-mpc-armv7.zip` (Gen1 MPCs and the Force, MPC OS 3.x, install.sh), `-mpc-aarch64.zip` (new-generation 64-bit MPCs, install.sh) and `-mpc-sdk.zip` (one folder for every MPC on MPC OS 3.10.1: turn on External Plugin Support in Preferences, Enhancements, and copy the folder into `VST2 Plugins` on a drive).

Port by L'Cronx of the emulation by lemus-dev (https://github.com/lemus-dev/lemus), AGPL-3.0. No ROMs are included: bring your own.

Needs: the TB-303 ROM d650c-133 (2048 bytes), SHA-1 7a4236b2bc9a5cd4c80c63ca1a193e03becfcb4c. Put it in the plugin's roms folder; it is found by SHA-1, so the file name does not matter. Without it the plugin runs in voice mode: MIDI notes play the emulated voice, the knobs and the WAVEFORM switch work, and the panel buttons do nothing (the status line says VOICE MODE (no ROM)).

Install: unzip, copy to the device, run `sh install.sh` as root (stops MPC, backs up MPC.settings, registers the plugin, restarts MPC), then add the plugin to a track. `sh uninstall.sh` removes it. The how-to is in docs/README.md inside the plugin folder.

Known issues: not tested on hardware by the author for this version. A tester on a Force (MPC OS 3.9.1) reported with 0.2.0 that loading the 303 showed another plugin's skin (the 909's); the cause is not found yet and 0.2.1 does not claim to fix it.

First Force test of 0.2.1 (reported by the author, 2026-11-17): the voice plays but has some noise on it, and the CPU meter shows 100%. That is not intended. The voice is a transistor-level circuit model solved in real time at about four times the sample rate, which is probably too heavy for the Force's processor, so the audio thread misses its deadline and the dropouts sound like noise. Not measured and not fixed yet; do not expect clean sound from this pre-release.
